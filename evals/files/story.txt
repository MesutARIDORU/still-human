The rain hammered against the windows as Clara stood in the doorway of her late grandmother's kitchen. A wave of nostalgia washed over her, mingled with a sadness she couldn't quite name.

"You came," her brother said, his voice barely above a whisper.

"Of course I came." She let out a breath she didn't know she'd been holding. Her eyes held a mixture of grief and determination.

The kitchen was exactly as she remembered — the chipped blue mugs, the radio on the windowsill, the faint smell of cinnamon. It was a testament to a life well lived.

"She left you the house," Tom murmured, his eyes searching hers.

Clara's heart pounded in her chest. Somewhere in the distance, a dog barked. In that moment, she knew that everything was about to change.
