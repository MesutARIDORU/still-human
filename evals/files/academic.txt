Social media platforms play a pivotal role in shaping adolescent wellbeing in today's digital age. This study delves into the multifaceted relationship between daily screen time and self-reported sleep quality among 412 secondary school students in two urban districts. Data were collected through an online questionnaire and analysed using multiple regression. Findings indicate that screen time after 10 p.m. was associated with lower sleep quality scores (β = −0.31, p < .01), while total daily screen time was not a significant predictor. These results underscore the importance of evening media habits and highlight the need for comprehensive, holistic interventions. It is important to note that the cross-sectional design does not allow causal inferences. Ultimately, this research contributes valuable insights to the ever-evolving landscape of adolescent health research, paving the way for future studies.
